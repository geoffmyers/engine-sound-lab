    Why engines sound different

    What you hear is mostly the exhaust: a burst of hot gas each time an exhaust valve opens. The firing frequency is rpm ÷ 60 × cylinders ÷ 2, so an inline-4 at 3,000 rpm pulses 100 times a second and a V8 200 times.

    Timing matters as much as count. A cross-plane V8 fires evenly overall, but each bank fires at 90-180-270-180° gaps, and that unevenness reaching each pipe is the burble. A flat-plane V8 alternates banks, so each pipe sees even pulses and it screams like two inline-4s.

    How this synthesizer works

    The crank angle advances sample by sample. At each cylinder's firing angle a pressure pulse is made, shaped by rpm, load and cylinder size, and delayed by that cylinder's header length. Pulses run through the collector, a turbine if fitted, a muffler chamber and a tailpipe modelled as a delay line whose open end reflects the wave back.

    Intake pulses ring the airbox, valves click, a turbo whistles and vents through its blow-off valve, a blower whines at its lobe frequency. A small physics model handles inertia, idle control, the rev limiter, fuel cut on the overrun and gear changes.

    In Drive, the engine turns a clutch, a gearbox with your choice of helical or straight-cut gears, and a final drive into a car with mass, drag and brakes. Gear whine sits at shaft speed times the teeth meshing; a straight-cut box screams, a helical one hums, and the direct-drive gear of a car box is nearly silent. Shifts sound like their hardware: a manual's clutch, synchro and clunk, a dog box's ignition cut and clack, a dual-clutch's quick handover.

    The listening position sets what reaches you: the balance of exhaust, intake and mechanical sound, a ground reflection, a gentle tone tilt and a little room. Three positions are fitted to recordings of a real BMW S65 (behind the car, at the intake, in the engine bay), as are its cold-start idle, the clean idle harmonics and the cycle-to-cycle variation. The driver's seat is estimated.
